Built to keep itself up to date

A key priority is a record your team can keep current with as little typing as possible.

The aim

Most broker systems go stale because keeping them current takes too much typing, and it feels like typing for someone else's report. After a few months people stop, and the system stops earning its keep.

Rhythm Bound is designed to work from what's already happening: your email, documents and calendar, the tone of what clients write, and well-established patterns in how working relationships grow and fade. You'll still type some things. The aim is to keep that to a minimum.

What you still do

  • Confirm a new client the first time it appears.
  • Check values it wasn't sure about, and correct anything it got wrong. A correction is used everywhere that value appears, so you make it once.
  • Add what only you know: a note after a call, a judgement about a client.
  • Approve what goes to clients and carriers.

Service

Mid-term changes

A client mentions a new truck in an email, or you photograph its plate on a site visit. It becomes a draft vehicle and a change request on the policy. The request, the carrier's acceptance and the effective date are tracked separately, so nothing shows as insured before it is.

Certificates

A client emails asking for a certificate for a new contract. It's drafted from the cover in force on that date, with the other party named. If they send the contract, its insurance requirements are checked against their cover before they sign. Every certificate is logged, so you know who holds one when the policy changes.

Claims

Claims are logged against the policy and the vehicle or item involved. The loss history is ready when the renewal comes round, and a declined claim brings the relationship to your attention.

Renewals

A renewal opens at 120 days. At 90, the client gets last year's schedule and one question: what's changed? At 60 you decide whether to renew as is or go to market. The renewal opens on what changed, with last year's figures beside this year's.

Additional coverage

When the record shows something the client owns but doesn't insure with you, such as equipment on the schedule with no equipment cover, it's suggested as an opportunity on that field of play. You decide whether to raise it. The quote comparison grid is shown in the overview.

    Intake and Today

    Most of a broker's day arrives by email. Intake picks it up in three ways, and Today turns it into a short list of things to do.

    Most of the work

    Your mailbox

    Connect your Gmail or Microsoft 365 account and Rhythm Bound reads client and carrier mail as it comes in. Each email is filed to the right client. Questions and requests are pulled out and tracked until someone answers them. If you wrote "I'll send that Friday", you get a reminder on Friday.

    When you want it dealt with now

    Forwarding

    Forwarding is the quickest way to start a new client. Send their first email to your intake address with "new client" and the client file is drafted for you to confirm. Write "this is for Lakeshore Paving" and it's filed there instead.

    From the client

    Replies and uploads

    Each client gets its own reply address, so their answers land in their file. Clients can also send documents through a secure upload link. They don't need an account or a password.

    Commission and fees

    Know what you should earn and check what you were paid. Your accounts stay in your accounting software.

    Commission

    Rates are stored by carrier and line, and expected commission is worked out on every policy and change. Carrier statements, PDF or spreadsheet, are matched line by line. Anything short, over or missing is listed with the calculation beside it.

    Fees and premium

    Broker fees are recorded separately from commission. For each policy you can see whether the carrier bills the client or you collect, and what's owed, received and passed on. Clients who want to pay monthly can be referred to premium financing.

    Your accountant

    Summaries go to QuickBooks or Xero, so nobody keys them in twice. Contingent commission targets show progress on volume, growth and loss ratio for each carrier.

    In the field

    The phone app is for site visits, yards and quick client details.

    Photos that file themselves

    Photograph equipment, serial plates and premises. Each photo goes to the right client and item. A photo of a plate fills in make, model and serial for you to confirm.

    Notes in your own words

    Dictate a note after a meeting. It's kept as you said it, and follow-ups are pulled out beside it. A new truck you mention becomes a suggested change on the policy.

    Before you walk in

    Check the client's file, open questions and renewal date. Approve and send emails from the phone. It works without signal and catches up when you're back online.

    Getting started

    What a brokerage sets up in the first weeks.

    Your name on everything

    Client and carrier emails go out from your domain, with your signatures and your logo.

    Your people

    Add your team and set who can bind. Anyone set up to need approval can do everything else, and their final step sends the bind to someone who can approve it.

    Your existing book

    Send your policy list and the documents for clients you already have. Rhythm Bound reads them, builds each client file and puts every renewal in the calendar.

    Your carriers

    Add the carriers you work with and your commission rates for each line.

    Each person chooses whether to connect their mailbox and calendar. The more people connect, the fuller the picture of who's in touch with each client.

    B. How the readings are worked out

    Strength

    Meeting, lunch or site visit3 points
    Phone call2 points
    Email1 point
    Contact the client startedhalf as much again
    • A contact's points halve every three months, so recent contact counts most.
    • Points become a figure from 0 to 100, so a few more emails make little difference to a strong relationship and a real difference to a weak one.
    • Each relationship is compared with what's normal for a client of that size and a person in that role.

    Coverage, health and direction

    • Coverage counts the key people in the field of play who have had contact with anyone at your firm in the last six months.
    • Health is the strength of each key person's relationship, weighted by their role. A key person with no contact counts as zero, and shows as a critical gap. For Lakeshore Paving: Tom 78 × 3, Lena 57 × 2, Priya 0 × 2, which gives 50.
    • Direction compares health with 90 days ago. A change of more than 5 points reads as stronger or weaker, otherwise steady. The reason is always shown.

    Sentiment

    • Read only from what the client writes. Your staff's messages are never read for tone.
    • Each reading shows the sentence it came from, and is attached to that message, never to the person.
    • Shown beside health, and doesn't change the number. A strained message is a reason to look, with the evidence in front of you.

    When there isn't enough to go on

    • With too little history, no reading is shown. It says "Not enough history yet".
    • Every reading is labelled as the system's reading, separate from facts on file.
    • Strain events, such as a declined claim, a large premium increase or a missed certificate deadline, are named in the reasons.

    C. Principles for relationship dynamics

    Know the right people. Know if relationships are healthy. Know when to act. Know when to leave things alone.

    What we want

    • Right relationships, not more relationships. We don't need everyone talking to everyone. We need commercially appropriate coverage.
    • Importance before activity. A contract signer matters differently from an operational contact. Understand who buys, decides, influences, signs, and uses the service.
    • Quality over quantity. Twenty emails don't necessarily beat one meaningful meeting.
    • Context before judgment. Infrequent contact isn't automatically bad. Different relationships require different levels of engagement.
    • Spot meaningful gaps. No relationship with a critical decision-maker may matter. No relationship with someone irrelevant may not.
    • Show what changed. Relationships can strengthen, weaken, or stay stable. Explain why.
    • Recommend sensible actions. Who should do what, with whom, and why? Sometimes the correct recommendation is to do nothing.
    • Evidence before conclusions. Show actual meetings, calls, emails, timelines, sentiment, and client feedback. Distinguish facts from AI inferences.
    • Use the whole firm's intelligence. Someone outside the account team may already have the relationship we need.

    What we don't want

    • No nagging clients just to increase contact frequency.
    • No treating every empty relationship cell as a problem.
    • No rewarding employees for sending more emails or making more calls.
    • No assuming a quiet relationship is an unhealthy relationship.
    • No single opaque score pretending to explain everything.
    • No AI inventing sentiment, relationship strength, or commercial importance without evidence.
    • No recommendations to contact the weakest relationship simply because it's the weakest.
    • No turning relationship dynamics into employee surveillance or performance rankings.
