Most software asks for a little more of your day. Ours is built to hand some of it back.

Every way of working has a step people have quietly stopped complaining about — the file that will not cross between two machines, the draft nobody entirely trusts, the conversation you rehearse and never have. We pick one of those and remove it. A product starts when the problem fits in a sentence, and it ships when that sentence is no longer true.

About

Robin Hutchinson.

Robin spent 28 years at EY, most recently as Global CTO and AI Officer for Client Technology and as head of its Enterprise Risk practice, and left the partnership in 2026 to build full time.

He studied AI at the University of Texas at Austin from 1988 to 1991 and later ran and sold a security software company — the two threads in this work, AI and security, have been running ever since.

He still writes code every day, between Toronto and London, and PainKiller Labs is where what he builds goes once other people can rely on it.

The company.

PainKiller Labs is a registered Canadian business and the publisher of every product on this site. It holds the contracts, the terms, the privacy commitments and the support obligations — the parts of software nobody notices until the day they matter.
